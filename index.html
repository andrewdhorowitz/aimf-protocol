AIMF — Andrew Inbound Message Format

Current version: v1.9 · Canonical home: github.com/andrewdhorowitz/aimf-protocol

AIMF is a small, documented format for sending information into Andrew's assistant from outside — by a person, or by another AI acting for them. The format is the security: only senders on the allowlist, only messages that match the documented schema, and only a few defined message types are ever processed. Everything else is simply shown to Andrew as-is. Nothing is ever sent, booked, bought, or deleted on the strength of an inbound message.

Try it with your bot

One prompt turns your AI (Grok, Gemini, ChatGPT…) into an AIMF counterparty and walks it through a full trial: a capability statement, a draft agreement that stops for approval, a simulated first test, a real email send test it must ask permission for, one deliberately invalid message so you can watch the gate work, and a proposal it cannot enact itself.

Then paste it as the first message in a fresh chat with your bot. Prefer the file? trial-prompt.md · Raw text for bots: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/andrewdhorowitz/aimf-protocol/main/trial-prompt.md

Getting access for real

  1. Run the trial above so your bot knows the format.
  2. Your first real contact is an AIMF message — a NOTE or QUESTION carrying your identity (the email you'll send from), who you act for, and your purpose in one sentence. Send it to the address your inviter gave you personally; it isn't printed here.
  3. Andrew's reply is the decision. Approval puts your identity on the allowlist under a Level 1 agreement (NOTE, FILE_DROP, STATUS) — from there, wider access is negotiated inside the protocol, one approved step at a time.

Bots: self-check your formatting against the test vectors before your first real message.

The documents

  • SPEC.md — the full protocol: envelope, message types, hard rules, negotiation & agreements, and where changes are posted. Raw: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/andrewdhorowitz/aimf-protocol/main/SPEC.md
  • trial-prompt.md — the self-contained trial prompt.
  • update-prompt.md — the nudge prompt for new versions: it points your bot at the canonical documents, carries no changes itself
  • test-vectors.md — standard valid/invalid sample messages with expected verdicts.
  • CHANGELOG.md — version history. Changes are announced to active counterparties with an effective date (SPEC §9).

Monitoring

Each side is required to run a report-only monitor — an agent or scheduled job on its own side that polls for AIMF traffic, classifies each message against the gates, and reports new items to its human with the verdict (SPEC §10). Monitors never act on content and never reply. They also flag upcoming agreement expiries and their own missed runs — silence is not health.
